Owls soar past Peacocks for third straight win

Temple outshot Saint Peter’s 22-4 in its 1-0 win Sunday afternoon.

23 August 2026Danny MezquitaFeatured, Soccer, Sports, Women's soccer

Temple and Saint Peter’s matchup was a typical early standoff with both teams trying to ease into the game Sunday afternoon. The Owls took a leap after cleaning up a botched Peacocks pass. Midfielder Valentina Bitterwolf took advantage of being in front of the penalty arc and launched the ball to break the tie. 

With this being the first time this season Temple had a first-half lead, it didn’t want to let up. It sent nine shots Saint Peter’s way while only defending three in the frame. The Owls played with force in the midfield and created eight corner kicks to stand their ground.

The final period didn’t sway the pendulum. Temple poured on 13 shots and kept the ball away from its net to secure its third consecutive win.

Temple (3-1, 0-0 American Conference) defeated Saint Peter’s (0-2, 0-0 Metro Conference) 1-0 Sunday afternoon at Temple Sports Complex. This is the Owls’ first three-game win streak since their 3-0 victory against Delaware State on Aug. 26, 2018.

“It’s very important to show presence, especially on our home turf,” Bitterwolf said. “It gives off more confidence, more power and better energy.”

The Owls notched the opening goal in the 17th minute when Bitterwolf pushed upfield and had the space to drill a long distance shot into the top of the net.

Temple was hungry for more after its first score. Bitterwolf sent a shot on goal less than two minutes later and the group added five more shots before halftime. The Owls also earned four corner kicks to the Peacocks’ zero. Temple was in complete control of the ball and consistently pressured the Saint Peter’s defense.

The Peacocks’ offense rarely saw possession. They only took three shots in the opening period and couldn’t regularly win battles for the ball. In turn, Temple goalkeeper Ana Zivkovic was hardly tested; she claimed crosses that came into her area but didn’t need to make any stops at her post.

Temple cruised into the locker room with a seven to zero shots on goal advantage.

The second half didn’t see a change in tempo. Temple stayed on the offensive attack and Saint Peter’s finally put one shot on target, but to no avail. The Owls sent five more attempts on goal and totaled 22 shots in the game.

Midfield possession was the difference maker. Temple consistently secured fifty-fifty balls before pushing downfield. Bitterwolf led the charge and won multiple aerial duels despite being one of the shortest players on the field.

In the Peacocks’ limited moments with avenues to the goal, they tried to send balls over the top of the Owls’ defense, but were unsuccessful. Midfielder Maddie Ward kept the unit in sync and stopped multiple passes from breaking through the back line.

“As a center back, I see the whole field and I like to talk,” Ward said. “I think it makes it easier for me because all my teammates know exactly what to do whether I’m talking or not.”

On the other end of the field, the Owls continued to press Peacocks’ goalkeeper Bergljot Kristinsdottir, who totaled eight saves. Temple added four more corner kicks in the second half to total eight on the day. But it didn’t capitalize on them, leaving points on the board. 

“A lot of set pieces is the service,” said head coach Chris Shaw. “And our service has been a little bit inconsistent.”

Still, Temple maintained authority for the remainder of the match to close its shutout.

Temple visits Rider (1-0-1, 0-0 Metro) on Aug. 27 at 7 p.m.

Danny Mezquita
