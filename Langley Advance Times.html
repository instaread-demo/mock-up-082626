New route links Langley Centre, Gloucester

Published 8:00 pm Tuesday, August 25, 2026

By Saman Dara

Starting on Sept. 7, the new 556 TransLink bus route will help connect Langley Centre to Aldergrove's Gloucester Industrial Area. (TransLink)

A new bus route will connect Langley City to the Gloucester Industrial Area in Aldergrove beginning Sept. 7, giving people heading to work a new way to get to one of the Township of Langley’s major employment centres.

TransLink announced its new 556 community shuttle, which is launching a year ahead of schedule.

The route includes 12 new bus stops in the Gloucester Industrial Area and will begin and end at Bay 1 at Langley Centre, near Logan Avenue.

The new bus route will operate Monday to Friday, with service every 30 minutes during morning and afternoon peak periods.

Buses will run to Gloucester from 5:15 to 8:15 a.m. and from 2:45 to 5:45 p.m.

Service to Langley Centre will run from 6 to 9 a.m. and from 3:30 to 6:30 p.m.

The service is part of TransLink’s 2025 Investment Plan and is expected to support more than 7,500 jobs over the next decade.

“We’re so excited to provide people with a direct, reliable transit connection to the Gloucester Industrial Area — giving new access to jobs and helping employers attract and retain the talent they need,” says TransLink CEO Kevin Quinn.

“By delivering this route a full year ahead of schedule, we’re supporting local workers and businesses faster, while strengthening our regional economy.”

The Greater Langley Chamber of Commerce also welcomed the new route.

“This is a major win for Gloucester businesses and employees,” said Chamber CEO Cory Redekop.

“For years, the lack of transit access made it harder for employers in Gloucester to recruit and retain workers, and harder for employees without access to a vehicle to consider the area for work. This new route is an important step forward.”

Gloucester is home to many large manufacturers, warehouses, food processors, and other businesses. The Chamber said it has been advocating for improved transit to the area for more than a decade.

Redekop described transportation as “economic infrastructure” that helps connect workers to jobs, businesses to labour, and employment areas to the broader regional economy.

“This route did not happen overnight,” Redekop said. “It took years of advocacy, persistence, partnership, and a clear message from the business community about the need for better transit access to one of Langley’s key job centres.”
